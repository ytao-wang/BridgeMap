# From Appearance to Structure: Bridging UAV Imagery and 2D Maps for Cross-View Geo-Localization
Cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) in GNSS-denied environments typically relies on satellite imagery as the reference source, but satellite data are costly to acquire and store and are sensitive to environmental variations. In contrast, 2D maps are lightweight, stable, and widely accessible, offering a promising alternative for scalable CVGL. However, their abstract, structure-dominant representation introduces a severe texture-to-structure gap with UAV imagery, while visually similar patterns may correspond to different semantic categories across views.  As a result, existing CVGL methods often significantly degrade when directly transferred to map-based references. To address these challenges, we propose BridgeMap, a novel multimodal map-based CVGL framework that combines structural modeling with semantic guidance. A training-only segmentation branch first encourages UAV features toward map-compatible geometric representations. We then introduce a Structure-Aware Mamba Block (SaMamba) that injects scan-aligned directional priors to model long-range geographic structures, together with a lightweight Language-Guided Alignment Module (LGAM) that uses class-level semantics to reduce visual ambiguity. We further construct two multimodal benchmarks, U1652-m and UVisloc-m, covering discrete and continuous localization, and introduce the real-world YangtzeMap benchmark under challenging conditions. Extensive experiments show that BridgeMap achieves state-of-the-art performance, improving R@1 by 20.14% on U1652-m and 7.28% on UVisloc-m over the strongest recent competitors.
